Podiatry and leg vein glossary

The words you will hear in a foot, ankle or vein appointment, explained in plain English. Each entry links to the page on this site that covers it in more depth.

  • A
  • B
  • C
  • D
  • E
  • F
  • H
  • L
  • M
  • O
  • P
  • R
  • S
  • T
  • V

A

Achilles tendinitis
Irritation and pain in the Achilles tendon, the thick cord that joins the calf muscles to the back of the heel. It is usually caused by overuse and is often worst with the first steps of the day. Also called: Achilles tendonitis. Read more
Achilles tendinosis
Long-standing wear inside the Achilles tendon, where the tissue has started to break down rather than heal. It tends to need treatment that restarts healing, not just rest. Read more
Ankle sprain
A stretch or tear of the ligaments that hold the ankle bones together, usually from rolling the foot inward. A sprain that keeps giving way or swelling weeks later should be examined. Read more

B

Board certified (podiatry)
A podiatrist who has passed the examinations of a recognized specialty board, such as the American Board of Foot and Ankle Surgery (ABFAS) or the American Board of Podiatric Medicine (ABPM). Read more
Bunion
A bony bump at the base of the big toe that forms when the big toe drifts toward the second toe and the joint behind it pushes outward. The medical name is hallux valgus. Also called: Hallux valgus. Read more
Bunionectomy
Surgery to correct a bunion. Most modern procedures realign the bone behind the big toe rather than only shaving the bump. Also called: Bunion surgery. Read more

C

Chronic venous insufficiency
A condition in which the one-way valves inside the leg veins stop closing properly, so blood pools in the lower leg. It causes swelling, aching, varicose veins and skin changes near the ankle. Also called: Venous insufficiency, CVI. Read more
Compression stockings
Elastic stockings that squeeze the leg most at the ankle and less higher up, helping blood move back toward the heart. They are often prescribed with or after vein treatment. Read more
Custom orthotics
Shoe inserts made from a mold or scan of your own feet to support or redistribute pressure. They differ from store-bought insoles, which are made to fit an average foot. Also called: Orthotics. Read more

D

Debridement
Removing dead or unhealthy tissue from a wound so the healthy tissue underneath can heal. Read more
Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)
A blood clot in a deep vein, usually in the leg. A painful, swollen, warm leg, especially on one side, needs urgent medical care, because a clot can travel to the lungs. Also called: DVT.
Diabetic foot ulcer
An open sore on the foot of a person with diabetes, often on the sole, that can go unnoticed when nerve damage has dulled sensation. Any wound on a diabetic foot should be seen promptly. Read more
DPM (Doctor of Podiatric Medicine)
The medical degree held by podiatrists, who diagnose and treat conditions of the foot, ankle and lower leg, both with and without surgery. Also called: Podiatrist, Doctor of Podiatric Medicine. Read more
Duplex ultrasound
A painless scan that shows the veins and the direction blood is flowing in them. It is the standard test for mapping which veins are leaking before vein treatment. Read more

E

Endovenous laser treatment (EVLT)
A minimally invasive vein procedure in which a thin laser fiber is placed inside a faulty vein through a needle puncture and used to seal it closed. Blood then reroutes through healthy veins. Also called: EVLT, Endovenous laser ablation. Read more

F

Fat pad atrophy
Thinning of the natural cushions under the heel and the ball of the foot, which can make standing on hard floors feel like walking on bone. Read more
First MTP joint
The joint at the base of the big toe, where the first metatarsal meets the toe. It is the joint involved in bunions and in big toe arthritis (hallux rigidus). Also called: First metatarsophalangeal joint, Big toe joint. Read more
Flatfoot
A foot whose arch is low or collapses when standing. Many flat feet cause no trouble; some cause arch, ankle or Achilles pain and benefit from support or treatment. Also called: Flat feet, Pes planus. Read more

H

Hallux rigidus
Arthritis of the big toe joint that makes the toe stiff and painful to bend, especially when pushing off to walk. Also called: Big toe arthritis. Read more
Hammertoe
A toe that bends downward at the middle joint, so the top of the toe rubs on shoes. It can be flexible early on and become rigid over time. Also called: Hammer toe. Read more
Heel spur
A small calcium deposit on the underside of the heel bone, seen on X-ray. Many people have one without pain; heel pain usually comes from the soft tissue around it. Read more
High ankle sprain
An injury to the ligaments that hold the two lower leg bones together just above the ankle. It usually takes longer to recover from than a common ankle sprain. Also called: Syndesmotic sprain. Read more

L

Loss of protective sensation
Reduced feeling in the feet, usually from nerve damage, to the point where a person may not notice a blister, cut or pressure sore. It is checked with a simple monofilament test. Also called: LOPS. Read more

M

Microphlebectomy
Removal of bulging surface varicose veins through tiny skin nicks, usually under local anesthetic. Also called: Ambulatory phlebectomy.
Minimally invasive bunion surgery
Bunion correction performed through several small incisions with guidance from live X-ray, instead of one long incision. Also called: MIS bunion surgery. Read more
MLS laser therapy
A treatment that uses a robotic, dual-wavelength laser on the skin over an injured area to reduce pain and inflammation. Sessions are short and painless. Also called: Multiwave Locked System laser. Read more
Monofilament test
A quick foot exam in which a thin nylon filament is pressed against several spots on the sole to check whether protective sensation is still present. Read more
Morton's neuroma
A thickened, irritated nerve between two toes, most often the third and fourth, causing burning or the feeling of a pebble in the shoe. Also called: Neuroma.

O

Offloading
Taking pressure off a wound or injured area of the foot with special boots, padding, casts or footwear so it can heal. Read more

P

Peripheral neuropathy
Damage to the nerves outside the brain and spinal cord, which in the feet causes burning, tingling, numbness or pain. Diabetes is the most common cause. Also called: Neuropathy, Diabetic neuropathy. Read more
Plantar fascia
The thick band of tissue under the foot that runs from the heel to the toes and supports the arch. Read more
Plantar fasciitis
Pain where the plantar fascia attaches to the heel, classically sharpest with the first steps in the morning or after sitting. It is the most common cause of heel pain. Read more
Plantar wart
A wart on the sole of the foot caused by a virus (HPV). Pressure from walking pushes it inward, which is why it can hurt like a stone in the shoe. Also called: Verruca plantaris, Verruca. Read more
Platelet-rich plasma (PRP)
A concentrate made from a small sample of your own blood, spun to collect the platelets, and injected into an injured tendon, ligament or fascia to support healing. Also called: PRP. Read more

R

Radiofrequency ablation (RFA)
A minimally invasive vein procedure that uses radiofrequency heat delivered through a thin catheter to seal a leaking vein closed. Also called: RFA, Radiofrequency vein ablation. Read more
Regenerative medicine
Treatments aimed at helping damaged tissue repair itself, rather than only masking pain. In foot and ankle care this includes laser, shockwave and PRP. Read more
Routine foot care (Medicare)
Medicare's term for nail trimming, corn and callus removal, and hygienic care. Medicare usually does not cover it, with exceptions for certain medical conditions. Read more

S

Sclerotherapy
Injection of a medicine into spider veins or small varicose veins that makes them close and fade over the following weeks. Read more
Shockwave therapy (EPAT)
A non-invasive treatment that sends acoustic pressure waves into a chronically injured tendon or fascia to restart the body's healing response. Also called: EPAT, ESWT, Extracorporeal shockwave therapy. Read more
Spider veins
Small red, blue or purple veins that sit flat under the skin in web-like clusters. They are usually painless; sometimes they signal a leaking vein underneath. Also called: Telangiectasia. Read more

T

Therapeutic shoes (Medicare)
Extra-depth or custom-molded shoes and inserts that Medicare Part B can cover once a year for people with diabetes and severe diabetic foot disease, when the required doctors certify and order them. Also called: Diabetic shoes. Read more

V

Varicose veins
Enlarged, twisted veins that bulge above the skin, usually on the legs. They often ache or throb and are a common sign of venous insufficiency. Read more
Vein ligation
A surgical procedure that ties off a faulty vein so blood reroutes through healthy veins. Read more

These definitions are general education, not a diagnosis. If a term here describes something you are dealing with, the next step is an exam. Call (561) 915-1934 or request an appointment at our Jupiter or Palm Beach Gardens office.
