The seven usual suspects

Where your heel hurts, and when, narrows the list fast:

ConditionWhere it hurtsTelltale pattern
Plantar fasciitisBottom of heel, toward the archStabbing first-step pain in the morning
Achilles tendonitisBack of heel / cord above itStiffness starting activity; worse with hills & sport
Heel spur syndromeBottom of heelUsually rides along with fasciitis; spur itself rarely the culprit
Retrocalcaneal bursitisBack of heel, deepSqueeze tenderness; aggravated by stiff shoe backs
Baxter's nerve entrapmentInner heelBurning quality; can mimic stubborn fasciitis
Fat pad atrophyCenter of heelDeep bruise feeling; worse barefoot on hard floors
Calcaneal stress fractureWhole heelPain with every step, worse over weeks; squeeze test positive

This is why "I tried inserts and they didn't work" is so common: inserts treat some of these well and others not at all. An exam by a foot and ankle specialist sorts this out in one visit, often with in-office imaging the same day.

How we treat heel pain

Once the cause is confirmed, treatment is matched to it, typically starting with the least invasive effective option:

  • MLS laser therapy for fasciitis, bursitis, and nerve irritation, painless and fast.
  • Shockwave therapy for chronic fasciitis and insertional tendon problems.
  • Orthotics and footwear strategy when mechanics are driving the overload.
  • Targeted stretching and strengthening matched to your diagnosis.
  • PRP or other regenerative options for resistant cases.
  • Immobilization for stress fractures, the one heel problem where rest truly is the treatment.
