5 Myths About Laser Therapy for Foot Pain, Debunked

Reviewed by Dr. Orlando Cedeno, DPM · 6 min read

MLS laser therapy is one of the most misunderstood treatments we offer, and the misunderstandings are what keep people in pain longer than they need to be. Here are the five we hear most often, and what's actually true.

Myth 1: It's painful

The truth: Laser therapy is painless. There are no needles, no incisions, and no recovery downtime. Most patients describe a mild warming sensation over the treated area and nothing more — some find the sessions genuinely relaxing. The confusion usually comes from lumping it in with heat-based or surgical treatments it has nothing in common with.

Myth 2: It's only for severe or chronic cases

The truth: It's used across the whole range, from early-stage plantar fasciitis to arthritis pain that's been building for years. If anything, starting earlier tends to work better — treating a problem before it becomes chronic generally means fewer sessions and a faster result than waiting until conservative care has already failed.

Myth 3: It doesn't actually work

The truth: This is the myth worth taking seriously, because skepticism about a device that just "shines light on your foot" is reasonable on its face. But the mechanism is well documented: photobiomodulation increases cellular ATP production, which drives faster tissue repair, while the laser simultaneously reduces inflammation and improves local blood flow. For plantar fasciitis, Achilles tendonitis, and arthritis pain specifically, that combination has real clinical support. It isn't a placebo effect from a fancy-looking machine.

Myth 4: It's unsafe

The truth: MLS laser therapy is FDA-cleared, non-invasive, and drug-free. It doesn't involve ionizing radiation the way an X-ray does — it uses specific light wavelengths that interact with tissue at a cellular level, with no significant side effects reported in clinical use. For patients trying to avoid another round of oral anti-inflammatories or a cortisone shot, it's often the lower-risk option, not the riskier one.

Myth 5: It's too expensive to be worth it

The truth: Cost depends on your condition and how many sessions it needs, and many insurance plans provide at least partial coverage. But the more useful way to think about cost is what the alternative actually costs: months of pain that limits activity, repeated cortisone injections that lose effectiveness over time, or eventually a surgery that a course of laser therapy might have prevented. We check your benefits and walk through real numbers before you commit to anything.

What it actually treats well

MLS laser therapy has the strongest track record for plantar fasciitis, Achilles tendonitis, and arthritis-related joint pain — conditions driven by inflammation and tissue damage that respond to accelerated cellular repair. It's not a universal fix for every kind of foot pain, and we'll tell you plainly if your specific problem is better served by something else. For the mechanism in more detail, see our MLS laser therapy page.
