Condition pages: doctor review

12 new condition pages are built. 12 are waiting for review. A page stays hidden from Google and off the Conditions page until a doctor has read it.

Open each page, read it as a patient handout, and check the list under its name. Send corrections to Nick. When a page is right, tell Nick which doctor reviewed it and the date; one line in the build publishes it with "Medically reviewed by" and your name.

Corns and Calluses · Waiting for a doctor (hidden from Google)

Open the page · 6 sources

Please confirm:

  • Diabetic warning is sourced to ACFAS (avoid home removal and medicated corn pads, serious infection may occur), Mayo Clinic (see a provider before self treating with diabetes or poor blood flow; avoid pumice stones if diabetic; salicylic acid risk) and NHS (do not self treat with diabetes, circulation problems, weakened immune system or older age; do not cut corns yourself). It appears in self-care, see_a_doctor, the intro and two FAQs.
  • Please confirm the practice offers routine corn and callus paring, padding, custom orthotics and in-office salicylic acid application (none are on the listed services). Also confirm whether Medicare routine foot care rules should be mentioned for diabetic patients; nothing about coverage was written.
  • Fact-check: the soft corn FAQ was rewritten to what AAOS OrthoInfo states (toes rubbing against each other; can resemble open sores; lamb's wool, roomy toe box). The earlier moisture and whitish-color wording was unsourced and removed.
  • NHS is a UK source; its guidance was used only for general self-care and red flags, not for UK-specific referral pathways.
  • ICD-10 L84 'Corns and callosities' (verified, billable).
  • Fact-check: first_visit no longer implies trimming happens at the first visit (BRIEF: do not invent same-day procedures). Confirm whether corns are typically pared at the first visit.
Gout in the Foot and Big Toe · Waiting for a doctor (hidden from Google)

Open the page · 5 sources

Please confirm:

  • Please confirm the doctors offer corticosteroid joint injections and joint aspiration for gout (not on the listed services). The ACFAS page refers to 'injections' generally.
  • The page says the podiatrist 'may order' blood work, imaging or joint fluid testing, since in-office lab or imaging is not a listed service. Confirm wording.
  • Long term uric acid lowering is framed as coordinated with primary care or rheumatology. Adjust if the doctors manage allopurinol or similar themselves.
  • ICD-10 M10.9 is 'Gout, unspecified' (verified, billable). More specific foot codes (for example idiopathic gout of the ankle and foot) were not verified and are not used.
  • Mayo Clinic describes pain peaking in the first 8 to 12 hours; NIAMS says flares last a week or two; ACFAS says 3 to 10 days with treatment. All three are attributed separately in the text.
  • Fact-check: the self-care tip to use a cool pack during a flare is not stated in the cited ACFAS, NIAMS or Mayo pages (ACFAS says rest and elevate). It was kept as conservative general advice; remove it if you prefer only sourced self-care.
Haglund's Deformity (Pump Bump) · Waiting for a doctor (hidden from Google)

Open the page · 4 sources

Please confirm:

  • ICD-10 left null: there is no single dedicated Haglund's code; practices variously use M77.9, M89.8X7, or M76.6- with M71.57- for retrocalcaneal bursitis. Please supply your preferred code.
  • EPAT is supported in the cited source (OrthoInfo) for insertional Achilles tendinitis, which commonly accompanies Haglund's, not for the bony bump itself. Please confirm the wording is acceptable.
  • Please confirm the practice offers orthotics, heel lifts, and casting for this condition; they are sourced standard options not on the service list.
  • The 2-week boot and up to 12-month rehab figures from OrthoInfo apply to Achilles debridement; reworded to attach them to cases that involve the tendon. Confirm this matches your surgical approach.
  • Cross-link with the existing Achilles tendonitis page.
  • Fact-check: the ACFAS URL with the encoded curly apostrophe (haglund%E2%80%99s-deformity) was fetched and returns HTTP 200 with the Haglund's page, so it was left unchanged. The APMA (/heelpain) and AAOS (/en/...) URLs redirected and were updated to their canonical addresses.
  • Fact-check: AAOS supports shockwave therapy for Achilles tendinitis only cautiously ('more information is needed before strong recommendations'); the EPAT method now carries that caveat. Doctor to confirm this framing is acceptable for marketing EPAT on this page.

Ingrown Toenail · Waiting for a doctor (hidden from Google)

Open the page · 6 sources

Please confirm:

  • Please confirm the practice performs partial nail avulsion and permanent edge removal (chemical matrixectomy, for example phenol) in the office. These are described as standard podiatric care attributed to ACFAS, APMA and MedlinePlus, not as a listed practice service.
  • FAQ 6 says the doctors 'perform nail procedures when needed'. Please confirm this wording.
  • what_it_is cites Cleveland Clinic's figure that two in every ten people who see a provider for a foot issue come in for an ingrown toenail. Keep or remove at your discretion.
  • ACFAS states most people resume normal activity the day after surgery. Please confirm this matches your aftercare instructions (soaks, dressings, shoe restrictions).
  • ICD-10 L60.0 (Ingrowing nail) verified as billable on icd10data.com (2027 edition).
  • The AAD ingrown toenail page returned a 404 when fetched, so it is not cited.
  • Fact-check (Oct 2026): Cleveland Clinic does not describe trimming for mild cases, and ACFAS warns that repeated trimming of nail borders can worsen the condition, so method 1 was relabeled to in-office lifting of the nail edge. Confirm how the doctors handle mild cases.
  • Fact-check: ACFAS home care says room-temperature soaks; Cleveland Clinic says warm water with Epsom salt twice daily. The page follows Cleveland Clinic with attribution. Confirm your preferred soak instructions.

  • Fact-check: the Mayo Clinic diagnosis URL ending drc-20354796 redirects to drc-20354795; the source list now uses the canonical URL.
Morton's Neuroma · Waiting for a doctor (hidden from Google)

Open the page · 4 sources

Please confirm:

  • This page quotes AAOS outcome figures (conservative care up to about 50%, steroid injections about 50 to 70%, surgery 80 to 95%, recurrence 5 to 20%). They are national figures attributed to AAOS, not practice outcomes. Remove them if you prefer no percentages on the page.
  • Please confirm the practice offers metatarsal padding, custom orthotics and cortisone or local anesthetic injections for neuroma. They are described as common podiatric options attributed to ACFAS.
  • Please confirm whether the surgeons perform neurectomy, decompression, or chemical or radiofrequency ablation. The page describes these generically per AAOS.
  • AAOS calls ultrasound the preferred imaging method. The page only says the doctor 'may order imaging' and makes no in-office ultrasound or X-ray claim.
  • MLS laser is omitted because no fetched source supports it for Morton's neuroma. Fact-check correction: AAOS OrthoInfo does mention extracorporeal shockwave therapy and platelet-rich plasma as second-line add-ons to other treatments, with limited research showing possible benefit. Doctor to decide whether to mention EPAT or PRP here, framed exactly that cautiously.
  • Cleveland Clinic states about 1 in 3 people have a Morton's neuroma at some point. This was left out as it reads high for a patient page.
  • ICD-10 G57.60 (Lesion of plantar nerve, unspecified lower limb) verified on icd10data.com; G57.6 lists Morton's metatarsalgia. Laterality codes are G57.61 (right), G57.62 (left), G57.63 (bilateral).
  • Fact-check: the cortisone FAQ previously said injections "relieve symptoms in about 50 to 70%" without the AAOS qualifier; it now says short-term relief, with long-term relief less common, as AAOS states.

  • Laser treatment for toenail fungus is intentionally not mentioned anywhere on this page, per the brief. Cleveland Clinic and AAD both reference laser; those lines were not used.
  • Please confirm the practice sends nail clippings to a lab for confirmation, and offers nail debridement and nail avulsion. These are described as standard options attributed to AAD and APMA.
