Symptom Guide

Where does it hurt?

Find the spot that matches your pain to see what's most commonly behind it — and how we treat it.

Heel

Stabbing heel pain — especially first steps?

Pain under the heel that's worst in the morning or after sitting is the signature of plantar fasciitis — the most common cause of heel pain. Heel spurs, stone bruises, and nerve entrapment can also be culprits.

  • Plantar fasciitis · Heel pain overview
Get It Evaluated
Arch

Aching or tearing pain through the arch?

Arch pain typically points to plantar fasciitis, posterior tibial tendon strain, or fallen arches. Left untreated, arch problems change the way you walk — and pass pain up to knees, hips, and back.

  • Plantar fasciitis · Strains
Get It Evaluated
Toes & Ball of Foot

Burning, tingling, or numbness in your toes?

Burning or "pins and needles" in the toes and ball of the foot is a hallmark of peripheral neuropathy — especially in patients with diabetes. Sharp forefoot pain can also be a neuroma or capsulitis; stiff, sore big toe joints often signal arthritis.

  • Neuropathy · Arthritis · Diabetic foot care
Get It Evaluated
Achilles & Back of Ankle

Pain or stiffness above your heel?

Pain along the cord at the back of your ankle is usually Achilles tendonitis — an overuse injury common in runners, tennis players, and weekend warriors. Chronic cases develop scar tissue that responds especially well to laser and shockwave therapy.

  • Achilles tendonitis · Sports injuries
Get It Evaluated
Ankle

Swollen, unstable, or aching ankle?

Ankle pain after a twist is a sprain — and sprains that never fully healed are a leading cause of chronic instability. Deep aching and stiffness with age or old injuries often means ankle arthritis.

  • Sprains & strains · Ankle arthritis
Get It Evaluated
Top of Foot

Soreness across the top of your foot?

Pain on top of the foot can be extensor tendonitis, a stress reaction from overuse, or midfoot arthritis. Athletes and anyone who's ramped up activity quickly are the usual suspects.

  • Sports injuries · Arthritis
Get It Evaluated
What To Expect

What laser treatment actually looks like

MLS laser therapy delivers two synchronized wavelengths deep into injured tissue, triggering your own cells to calm inflammation and rebuild — no heat damage, no needles, no recovery time.

How MLS Laser Works See our technology

  1. Evaluation & diagnosis

    We examine your foot and ankle, review imaging if needed, and pinpoint the true pain generator.

  2. Custom treatment plan

    Most conditions call for 6–12 laser sessions, 2–3 times per week — sometimes combined with shockwave or regenerative therapy.

    Effects are cumulative: circulation improves, tissue rebuilds, and results keep developing after your last session.

Good Questions

Quick answers before you call

Does laser therapy hurt?

No. MLS laser therapy is painless — most patients feel either nothing at all or a mild, soothing warmth. There are no needles, no incisions, and no recovery time. You walk in, relax for 10–15 minutes, and walk out.

How many sessions will I need?

Most conditions respond within 6–12 sessions, typically scheduled 2–3 times per week. Acute injuries often need fewer; chronic conditions like long-standing plantar fasciitis or neuropathy may need more. Dr. Cedeno gives you a specific plan after your evaluation — and adjusts it as you improve.

Is MLS laser therapy FDA cleared?

Yes. MLS laser therapy is FDA-cleared for the treatment of pain, inflammation, and to promote healing. It has been used in thousands of clinics worldwide with an excellent safety record and no significant reported side effects.

Will insurance cover my treatment?

Your evaluation and many podiatric services are typically covered by insurance. Laser and regenerative therapies are often elective; our team gives you exact pricing up front — no surprises. Call (561) 915-1934 and we'll verify your benefits before your first visit.
