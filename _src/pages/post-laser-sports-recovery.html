Laser Therapy for Sports Injuries: Faster Recovery

Reviewed by Dr. Orlando Cedeno, DPM · 5 min read

An athlete's injury clock runs differently — every week out matters. Laser therapy doesn't skip the biology of healing, but it does help tissue get through it faster, which is exactly why it's become a staple in sports medicine.

What it actually does

Laser therapy uses light energy to stimulate cellular activity — increasing the ATP production cells rely on to repair themselves, reducing inflammation, and easing pain. That combination is why it accelerates recovery rather than just masking discomfort while an injury heals on its own schedule.

What it treats well

  • Muscle strains — reduces the inflammation and pain that otherwise limits early movement and rehab.
  • Tendonitis — including Achilles tendonitis and plantar fasciitis, where repetitive strain has caused ongoing inflammation and tissue degeneration.
  • Ligament damage — supports the repair process in sprained or strained ligaments.
  • Post-surgical recovery — used alongside standard post-op care to support tissue healing and manage pain after foot or ankle surgery.

Why athletes reach for it

Three things make it a good fit for active people specifically: it's non-invasive with no needles, incisions, or medication; sessions typically run 10–15 minutes with zero downtime, so it fits around training and competition schedules; and because it directly targets tissue repair rather than only symptoms, it can shorten the real timeline back to activity, not just how comfortable the wait feels.

What it isn't

Laser therapy accelerates healing. It doesn't eliminate the need for it. The most effective recovery plans combine laser therapy with appropriate rest, a graduated return to activity, and often targeted rehab exercises — not laser therapy in place of any of those. Anyone promising laser alone as a complete fix for a significant injury is overselling one tool in a larger plan.

When to start

For an acute strain or ligament injury, starting laser therapy soon after the injury can help limit how much inflammation builds up before it's addressed. For a chronic, nagging issue that never fully resolved with rest alone — a common story with tendon injuries specifically — it's equally useful started later, once conservative measures alone have stalled.

Building a real recovery plan

The right combination of laser therapy, rest, and rehab depends on the specific injury, its severity, and your sport's demands. A treatment plan built around your actual injury and timeline gets you back faster than a generic protocol.
