What is minimally invasive bunion surgery?

Minimally invasive (MIS) bunion surgery corrects the bone alignment behind a bunion through several small incisions, often just a few millimeters each. The surgeon uses specialized instruments and watches the correction on live X-ray (fluoroscopy) instead of opening the joint to see it directly.

A bunion is a bone-alignment problem, not a bump sitting on top of the foot. The bone behind the big toe drifts one way and the toe angles the other. Lasting correction means realigning that bone. MIS does that work through smaller openings.

Who is a good candidate?

Many mild and moderate bunions are well suited to a minimally invasive correction. Some bunions do better with a different operation:

  • An arthritic big toe joint. If the joint surface itself is worn, realigning the bone won't relieve the pain. A joint procedure may be the right answer instead.
  • An unstable first metatarsal. When the bone is loose where it meets the midfoot, a Lapidus fusion is often the more durable fix. Our surgeons perform it when it is indicated.
  • A bunion that has come back after surgery. Revision bunion surgery is its own discipline, and it starts with understanding why the first correction failed.

Getting the choice of procedure right matters more than incision size. A board-certified foot & ankle surgeon examines the foot and reviews your X-rays, then recommends the least invasive operation that will hold. See our comparison of bunion surgery types for the full range.

How it differs from traditional bunion surgery

Minimally invasiveTraditional (open)
IncisionsSeveral small incisions, often a few millimeters eachOne longer incision over the joint
How the surgeon sees the boneLive X-ray guidanceDirect, open view of the joint
Tissue around the jointLess disruptedMore is opened to reach the bone
ScarringSmall scarsA longer scar along the joint
Bone healingThe same either way: bone heals on its own schedule, and about six weeks is a common milestone

That last row matters. Smaller incisions can mean less swelling and discomfort early on, but they do not make bone heal faster. Be wary of anyone who promises you'll be back in heels in a week.

From Consultation to Normal Shoes

What recovery looks like

  1. Consultation and X-rays

    An exam and weight-bearing X-rays show how far the bone has drifted and whether the joint is healthy. You leave knowing which procedure fits and why.

  2. Surgery day

    Bunion correction is outpatient surgery, and you go home the same day. Live X-ray guidance lets the surgeon confirm the correction before closing.

  3. Protected walking

    Most patients can bear some weight in a surgical shoe or boot within days. Your surgeon tells you exactly how much and when to increase it.

  4. Around six weeks

    About six weeks is a common bone-healing milestone, confirmed on follow-up X-rays. Many patients start moving back toward regular shoes from here.

  5. Back to full activity

    Returning to full activity is gradual and can take longer than six weeks, depending on the correction and how your foot heals.

Timelines vary by procedure and patient. Your surgeon sets weight-bearing and activity limits at every follow-up. For the day-to-day detail, read bunion surgery recovery tips.

MIS Bunion Q&A

Minimally invasive bunion surgery, answered

What is minimally invasive bunion surgery?

It corrects the bone alignment behind a bunion through several small incisions, often a few millimeters each, using specialized instruments guided by live X-ray. It aims for the same correction as traditional surgery with less disruption to the tissue around the joint.

Is it better than traditional bunion surgery?

For the right bunion it can mean less soft-tissue disruption, smaller scars and often less early swelling. It is not the right operation for every bunion. Severity, the condition of the big toe joint, stability of the first metatarsal and bone quality all decide which procedure will hold up best.

How long is recovery?

About six weeks is a common bone-healing milestone. Returning to normal shoes and full activity is gradual and often takes longer, depending on the correction performed and your health.

Can I walk after surgery?

Most patients can bear some weight in a surgical shoe or boot within days, though the exact timeline depends on the specific correction. Your surgeon tells you how much weight to put on the foot and when to increase it.

Is Bunionplasty the same thing?

Bunionplasty is one minimally invasive approach to bunion correction. MIS bunion surgery is the broader category. More in Is Bunionplasty Right for You?

Does insurance cover it?

We accept insurance and also offer self-pay options. Coverage depends on your plan and on medical necessity. Our insurance guide explains how to check your own plan before surgery is scheduled.
