Ankle Pain: Common Causes, Treatments and Preventions

Reviewed by Dr. Orlando Cedeno, DPM · 6 min read

The ankle carries your full body weight through every step, run, and jump — so when something goes wrong there, it affects everything else. Understanding what's actually causing the pain is the first step to treating it correctly.

Who's most at risk

Ankle pain and injury are more common in people over 65, those playing sports involving jumping, side-to-side movement, or quick direction changes, and people carrying excess weight, which adds extra load to the joint.

What commonly causes it

  • Sprains and strains — ligaments stretching or tearing from a sudden twist, or muscles/tendons overstretching around the joint.
  • Fractures — breaks in the ankle bones from falls, sports injury, or trauma, causing severe pain and difficulty bearing weight.
  • Tendonitis — inflammation from overuse, repetitive movement, or improper footwear.
  • Arthritis — osteoarthritis or rheumatoid arthritis causing chronic pain, stiffness, and swelling that worsens with activity.
  • Tarsal tunnel syndrome — nerve compression along the inner ankle, causing pain, numbness, tingling, or weakness.
  • Achilles tendon injury — from a partial tear to a full rupture, typically from sudden force or chronic overuse.

Treatment options

For acute injuries like sprains, strains, or fractures, rest and immobilization (a brace, splint, or walking boot) protect the joint while it heals. Ice and elevation in the first 48 hours reduce pain and swelling. Physical therapy rebuilds strength, flexibility, and range of motion once acute pain has settled. Medication — over-the-counter pain relievers or, in some cases, corticosteroid injections — helps manage pain and inflammation. Custom orthotics, ankle braces, or supportive footwear add stability for injured or arthritic ankles during daily activity. For severe cases that don't respond to conservative care, surgery may be needed to repair damaged ligaments, tendons, or bone.

Reducing your risk going forward

Regular strength, flexibility, and balance training targeting the ankle; supportive footwear appropriate to the activity; a proper warm-up with dynamic stretching before exercise; correct technique to avoid unnecessary stress on the joint; and gradually increasing training intensity rather than jumping in too fast all meaningfully lower the risk of ankle injury. Paying attention to early discomfort — rather than pushing through it — is often what keeps a minor issue from becoming a bigger one.
