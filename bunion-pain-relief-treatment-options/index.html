Bunion Pain Relief: Top Treatment Options

Reviewed by Dr. Orlando Cedeno, DPM · 6 min read

Surgery isn't the only answer for a bunion — for mild to moderate pain, a range of non-surgical options can bring real relief. Here's what actually works, and how to know when it's time to consider more.

Why bunions happen

A bunion forms when the big toe joint shifts out of alignment, creating a bony bump on the side of the foot that worsens gradually. Genetics, foot structure (flat feet or high arches), tight or narrow footwear, and inflammatory conditions like rheumatoid arthritis can all contribute. Common signs include the visible bump itself, redness and soreness around the joint, restricted toe movement, and pain that worsens with tight shoes or prolonged standing.

Non-surgical options that help

  • Proper footwear and orthotics — a wide toe box, low heels, and good arch support relieve pressure; custom orthotics help distribute weight more evenly.
  • Padding and taping — bunion pads or moleskin protect the area, while taping or splinting can help realign the toe and ease pain.
  • Ice and anti-inflammatory medication — 15–20 minutes of ice several times daily, paired with over-the-counter NSAIDs, reduces swelling and pain.
  • Stretching and strengthening exercises — toe stretches, towel scrunches, and marble pick-ups build flexibility and strength around the joint.
  • Physical therapy — stretching, massage, and ultrasound therapy delivered by a professional for more structured relief.
  • Night splints and correctors — hold the toe in a straighter position overnight, which may help slow progression; gel toe spacers ease daytime discomfort.
  • Cortisone injections — for more severe pain and inflammation, offering temporary relief without correcting the underlying bunion.

When conservative treatment isn't enough

Some cases need more than home management. It's worth seeing a podiatrist if pain persists despite conservative treatment, if walking or wearing shoes becomes genuinely difficult, if there's significant swelling or redness, or if the bunion is growing quickly. When conservative options are exhausted and pain is affecting mobility, surgical correction — realigning the joint and removing excess bone — becomes the more effective path forward.

Reducing the risk of it getting worse

Supportive, well-fitting footwear, maintaining a healthy weight, regular foot stretches, and catching early changes before they progress all help keep a bunion from advancing faster than it needs to. Genetics can't be changed, but the pace of progression often can.
