Laser Therapy for Ankle Sprains: Faster Recovery

Reviewed by Dr. Orlando Cedeno, DPM · 4 min read

An ankle sprain is a soft-tissue injury, and soft tissue heals on its own timeline — but that timeline isn't fixed. MLS laser therapy is aimed specifically at shortening it.

What's actually injured in a sprain

An ankle sprain happens when a sudden twist or misstep stretches or tears the ligaments supporting the joint — a common injury during exercise, sport, or just an awkward step off a curb. The result is pain, swelling, and often days to weeks of limited mobility while the ligament repairs itself.

How laser therapy works on a sprain specifically

MLS laser therapy uses two synchronized wavelengths of light that penetrate into the injured tissue. This FDA-cleared, clinically studied approach:

  • Reduces inflammation in the injured ligament and surrounding tissue directly.
  • Eases pain through the same mechanism, without medication.
  • Reduces swelling, which is often what limits mobility most in the first days after a sprain.
  • Stimulates cellular repair, giving the damaged ligament fibers more of what they need to rebuild.
  • Helps limit scar tissue formation, which matters for how the ankle feels and moves once it's healed.

What treatment is like

Non-invasive, no needles, sessions typically running 10–15 minutes. How many sessions depends on how severe the sprain is — a mild sprain needs fewer than a more significant ligament injury, and the plan is adjusted as the ankle actually progresses rather than following a fixed script.

Laser therapy alongside proper recovery

Laser therapy speeds the biology of healing. It doesn't replace the basics that still matter — appropriate rest early on, and a graduated return to activity once the ankle can tolerate it. Used together, most sprains resolve fully; laser therapy alone, without respecting the ankle's actual healing stage, won't get you back faster than pushing too soon would set you back.
