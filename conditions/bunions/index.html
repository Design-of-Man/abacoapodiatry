Bunion Treatment in Jupiter, FL

A bunion is unmistakable: a bony bump that pushes your big toe toward — or even atop — your smaller toes. They detract from your foot's appearance, but that's the least of it. Bunions can be genuinely painful and lead to poor mobility and difficulty wearing shoes.

What is a bunion?

A bunion is a joint deformity that causes a bony bulge on the side of your big toe. Bunions can cause soreness, pain, swelling, and other issues — including real embarrassment about the appearance of your feet.

Bunions grow progressively worse, so early treatment is the best way to avoid continued foot problems.

How does a bunion happen?

Bunions happen because bones in your forefoot shift, which affects the metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint that joins your big toe to the rest of your foot. As the forefoot bones exert pressure on the MTP joint, the joint gravitates inward toward your other foot.

The resulting bony bump grows bigger as the MTP joint drifts inward even more. This joint movement causes your big toe to lean outward, moving toward — and possibly over the top of — your second toe as the deformity worsens.

The cause of the forefoot changes that lead to bunions varies. Usually it's related to faulty foot structure, something you inherit from your parents. You can also develop a bunion after trauma, like dropping a heavy box on your foot.

Do tight shoes cause bunions?

Wearing toe-crowding shoes doesn't actually cause bunions — but it can certainly speed up bunion development if you already have a foot type that's vulnerable to them.

How do you treat bunions?

At Abacoa Podiatry & Leg Vein Center, Dr. Cedeno considers the extent of the bone changes in your foot, your activity level, your overall health, and other individual factors when formulating a treatment approach. Usually the first-line treatment is nonsurgical care to relieve your bunion pain:

  • Taping
  • Padding
  • Custom orthotics
  • Changing footwear
  • Anti-inflammatory medication

Conservative care is about symptom management rather than deformity correction. But if your bunion is very large, or causes pain that just isn't manageable with conservative care, Dr. Cedeno may recommend bunion surgery.

Bunion surgery

There are many different kinds of bunion surgery, including removing inflamed tissue, partial bone removal, forefoot bone realignment, and joint fusion. Dr. Cedeno designs a surgical plan to give you maximum relief and long-term foot health.

Our surgeons also offer minimally invasive (MIS) techniques where appropriate — smaller incisions designed to reduce postoperative discomfort and speed recovery.

Signs you should be seen

  • A visible bump at the base of your big toe
  • Big toe leaning toward the second toe
  • Pain or burning at the joint, especially in shoes
  • Redness, swelling, or callus over the bump
  • Shoes that used to fit now rub or hurt

Because bunions are progressive, the earlier you're evaluated, the more options you have.

Bunion Q&A

Bunion questions, answered

What is a bunion?

A joint deformity that causes a bony bulge on the side of your big toe, often with soreness, pain, and swelling. Bunions grow progressively worse, so early treatment is the best way to avoid continued foot problems.

Will my bunion go away on its own?

No — bunions are progressive structural deformities. Conservative care can control symptoms very effectively, but the bump itself won't reverse without surgical correction.

Do I have to have surgery?

Most patients don't. Taping, padding, custom orthotics, footwear changes, and anti-inflammatories manage the majority of bunions well. Surgery is reserved for bunions that are very large or painful beyond what conservative care can control.

Is bunion surgery painful? How long is recovery?

Recovery depends on which procedure your anatomy requires — from soft-tissue and partial bone procedures to full realignment or fusion. Dr. Cedeno walks you through the specific plan, timeline, and what weight-bearing looks like before you commit to anything. Minimally invasive options can further reduce postoperative discomfort in appropriate cases.

Can bunions come back after surgery?

Recurrence is possible, which is exactly why the choice of procedure matters. Surgery matched to your actual deformity — rather than a one-size-fits-all "bunionectomy" — is designed for long-term foot health, not just removing a bump.

My bunion doesn't hurt. Should I still be seen?

Worth a visit. A baseline evaluation tells you how your foot structure is trending and which simple changes (footwear, orthotics) can slow progression while it's still painless.
