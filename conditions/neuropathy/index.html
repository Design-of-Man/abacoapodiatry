Peripheral Neuropathy Treatment in Jupiter, FL

Burning feet at night. Tingling toes. Numbness that makes every step feel wrong. If you've been told your only option is a pill bottle refilled forever. There's more to the story.

What is peripheral neuropathy?

Peripheral neuropathy is damage to the small nerves farthest from your spine, and because your feet are at the end of the line, that's where it shows up first. Damaged nerves misfire (burning, tingling, electric jolts) or stop firing (numbness, loss of balance). Symptoms often start in the toes and creep upward in a "stocking" pattern, and they're usually worst at night.

Diabetes is the most common cause, roughly half of people with diabetes develop some neuropathy, but chemotherapy, vitamin deficiencies, thyroid conditions, certain medications, and idiopathic (unknown) causes are all common too.

The problem with the standard approach

The conventional playbook is nerve-pain medication: gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine. These can genuinely help, but they share a limitation: they quiet the pain signal without doing anything for the nerves themselves. Meanwhile side effects like grogginess, dizziness, and weight gain lead many patients to ask us for another way.

How MLS laser therapy fits in

MLS laser therapy takes a different approach: it targets the environment the nerves live in. Laser energy improves microcirculation, the tiny blood vessels that feed nerve tissue, and boosts cellular energy production in oxygen-starved tissue. For many patients this means less burning, less tingling, better sleep, and steadier feet. Treatment is painless, drug-free, and takes 10–15 minutes per session.

We'll be straight with you: laser therapy doesn't reverse every case, and severely damaged nerves may not recover. That's why we start with an honest evaluation, including screening for treatable causes, and track your response objectively over the first weeks of care.

Neuropathy warning signs

  • Burning or tingling, worse at night
  • Numb patches or "dead" toes
  • Electric or stabbing jolts
  • Feeling like you're walking on socks or pebbles
  • Balance trouble in the dark
  • Feet that don't feel hot or cold properly

FAQ

Neuropathy questions, answered

Can neuropathy be cured?

It depends on the cause and how early it's caught. Neuropathy from a treatable cause (B12 deficiency, thyroid issues, medication) can improve substantially when the cause is corrected. Diabetic neuropathy can be slowed, and symptoms often meaningfully reduced, with good glucose control plus supportive treatment. What's almost always true: earlier is better, because nerves still partially alive respond far better than nerves long gone.

How many laser sessions do neuropathy patients need?

Neuropathy is typically treated with a longer program than an acute injury, often 12+ sessions, because nerve tissue changes slowly. Dr. Cedeno sets expectations honestly at your evaluation and checks your response at defined milestones.

Do I need to stop my neuropathy medication for laser therapy?

No. Laser therapy works alongside your current medication. If your symptoms improve, any medication changes happen gradually and through the prescribing physician.

Why see a podiatrist for neuropathy instead of just my PCP?

Because neuropathy in the feet is both a symptom problem and a safety problem. Beyond treating discomfort, a foot specialist protects you from the complications numbness invites (unnoticed wounds, deformity, ulcers) with skin checks, footwear guidance, and preventive care your PCP visit doesn't have time for.
