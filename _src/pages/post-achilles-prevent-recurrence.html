Achilles Tendonitis Keeps Coming Back? Here's Why

Reviewed by Dr. Orlando Cedeno, DPM · 6 min read

Achilles tendonitis that keeps returning is almost never bad luck. It is the sign of a tendon that was never actually allowed to finish healing before activity resumed. Pain going away and tissue being repaired are two different events, and treating them as the same one is the single most common reason this condition relapses.

Why it keeps coming back

Achilles tendonitis is an overuse injury: repetitive stress to the tendon connecting your calf muscles to your heel bone. The trap is that pain typically fades well before the tendon has finished remodelling. Collagen repair continues for weeks to months after symptoms settle, and a tendon that feels fine can still be structurally behind where it needs to be to handle full load.

The common causes of recurrence are all versions of the same mistake:

  • Returning to running, sport, or heavy activity too soon after pain resolves
  • Footwear with poor support or no heel cushioning
  • Tight or weak calf muscles that keep loading the tendon unevenly
  • Training or gait mechanics nobody has actually corrected
  • Skipping the stretching or physical therapy program after the pain stops, because it stopped hurting

If none of those root causes gets addressed, the tendon heals into the same vulnerable state it was in before — and the next hard week of activity reinjures it.

The return-to-activity plan that actually prevents relapse

Go slow, on purpose

The most common single mistake is returning to full activity too fast. Use the 10% rule: increase training load by no more than 10% a week, build in rest days, and track any discomfort in a simple log rather than pushing through and hoping.

Fix the footwear

Shoes without real arch support or a slight heel lift add strain to a healing tendon. Choose footwear built for what you actually do — running shoes for running, not for standing all day — and ask about custom orthotics or a heel lift if support alone isn't cutting it.

Stretch and strengthen, consistently

Tight calves overload the tendon; weak calves can't support it properly either. A daily routine matters more than an occasional one:

  • Calf stretches, both straight-knee and bent-knee
  • Eccentric heel drops — slowly lowering the heel off a step, which has the strongest evidence behind it for tendon recovery
  • Toe walks and heel raises to rebuild ankle stability

For the exercises themselves in full and a phased plan to rebuild toward running or sport, see our guide to returning to activity after Achilles tendonitis.

Know when pain is a warning, not background noise

Mild tightness after activity is normal. Sharp pain, pain that changes your gait, or pain that returns every time you resume the same activity is your tendon telling you it isn't ready for that load yet. Treat it as information, not an inconvenience to push through.

When to stop managing it yourself

See a specialist if the pain has come back more than once, if it has lasted beyond six weeks, or if you notice thickening along the tendon, a nodule, or stiffness that takes more than a few minutes to work out in the morning. Those are signs the tendon has moved from simple inflammation toward tendinosis — a structural change that responds better to active treatment than to rest alone.
