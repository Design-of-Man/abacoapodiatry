# Photos we need from the practice

Six condition and service pages now carry explanatory diagrams, which is the right
fill for anatomy. What diagrams cannot do is show that this is a real office with real
people in it, and that is the half only the practice can supply.

**Every shot below does double duty on the Google Business Profile.** GBP is the one
account already accessible and the listing is thin, so a single session covers both
jobs. See `_dev/listings-nap.md`.

Phone photography is fine. Daylight, clean surfaces, landscape orientation, nothing
below 1600px wide. No patients in frame unless they have signed a release, and no
screens showing patient information.

## Worth having next

Ordered by how much each one earns, not by how easy it is.

1. **Treatment room, wide.** Goes on `/technology/` and the service pages. Patients
   want to see where they will be sitting.
2. **A treatment in progress**, laser head over a foot, no faces. The single most
   useful image on the site, and the one that makes the service pages concrete.
3. **Both doctors together.** `/about/` currently has portraits linked but no shot of
   the pair. Practices with two surgeons should look like it.
4. **Front desk and waiting area.** Reduces first-visit anxiety and is the image GBP
   surfaces most often in local results.
5. **Shockwave and ultrasound units.** The vein page claims ultrasound mapping and the
   shockwave page describes the device; showing them is corroboration.
6. **Palm Beach Gardens exterior.** The second location has its own page and its own
   GBP listing, and currently no image at all.

## Not worth doing

Stock photography of feet, generic clinicians, or laser beams. It reads as stock to
patients, it does nothing for the GBP listing, and search engines have seen every one
of those images on a hundred other podiatry sites.

Same answer for AI-generated clinical imagery. On a medical practice's site a
photograph is a claim about the room and the equipment, and that claim should be true.
