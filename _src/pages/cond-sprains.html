Sprain vs. strain, and why grading matters

A sprain is a stretched or torn ligament (the tissue connecting bone to bone, think rolled ankle). A strain is a stretched or torn muscle or tendon (think pulled calf). Both come in grades from mild overstretch (Grade 1) to complete tear (Grade 3), and the grade determines everything: how long you protect it, when you load it, and whether you need more than rehab.

Here's the statistic that should change how you treat your next sprain: a large share of significant ankle sprains, commonly cited at up to 40%, leave lingering symptoms or chronic instability when they're not properly rehabilitated. The ligament heals long and loose, position-sense reflexes stay dulled, and the ankle keeps giving way on curbs and courts for years.

How we treat sprains and strains

  1. Rule out the bad stuff

    Fractures, high-ankle sprains, and tendon ruptures hide inside "normal" sprains. A focused exam, with same-day imaging when indicated, catches them.

  2. Protect, then move early

    Modern sprain care is early protected motion, not weeks in a boot on the couch. We brace what needs bracing and get everything else moving.

  3. Accelerate healing with laser

    MLS laser therapy shines in acute injuries: it reduces swelling and pain quickly and speeds soft-tissue repair, useful from the first days after injury.

  4. Rebuild strength and balance

    Strength, range, and proprioceptive (balance) work is the part everyone skips, and the part that prevents the next sprain. We make it specific and short.

  5. Fix the repeat offenders

    Chronically unstable ankles get a dedicated plan: advanced rehab, bracing strategy, regenerative options for the ligament, and surgical repair when instability persists despite it all.
