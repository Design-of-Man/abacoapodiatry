Insurance & Coverage, Explained

Most people calling a podiatrist want one question answered first: is this covered? Here is the honest breakdown: what insurance usually pays for, what it usually doesn't, and how to find out exactly where your plan lands before you book.

Verify My Benefits: (561) 915-1934 Book Appointment
The Short Version

What insurance typically covers

Podiatry splits fairly cleanly into two buckets, and knowing which one your problem falls into tells you most of what you need to know.

  • Usually covered by insurance: your evaluation, imaging, and standard podiatric care, including diabetic foot care and most surgical procedures when needed.
  • Usually out-of-pocket: laser therapy, shockwave, PRP, and regenerative injections. Most insurers classify these as elective. We offset that with transparent package pricing you see before committing to anything.
  • HSA/FSA: physician-prescribed treatment for a medical condition is generally eligible: we provide the documentation.

If your foot hurts and you don't yet know why, the visit that finds out is the covered part. That is the appointment to book.

Medicare and Podiatry, 2026

Does Medicare cover podiatry?

Yes, when the care is medically necessary. Medicare Part B covers a podiatrist’s exams and treatment for foot injuries and diseases, such as hammertoes, bunion deformities and heel spurs. What it usually does not cover is routine foot care. Both of our physicians are credentialed with Medicare, and the rules below come straight from Medicare.gov and the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS).

What the 2026 numbers are

  • Part B deductible: $283 for 2026. You pay it once a year before Part B starts sharing costs.
  • Coinsurance: after the deductible, usually 20% of the Medicare-approved amount. Care in a hospital outpatient department also carries a copayment.
  • Standard Part B premium: $202.90 a month for 2026; higher incomes pay more.

These figures are set by CMS and change every January. A Medicare supplement (Medigap) policy may pay some or all of the 20%. Medicare Advantage plans cover the same services but set their own costs, networks and referral rules, so call the number on your card or call us and we will check.

Diabetic shoes: the paperwork that makes them covered

Medicare pays for therapeutic shoes only when three things line up. The doctor who treats your diabetes certifies that you need them. A podiatrist or other qualified doctor orders them. And you get them from a supplier enrolled in Medicare, such as a podiatrist, orthotist, prosthetist or pedorthist. If you think you qualify, bring the name of the doctor who manages your diabetes to your diabetic foot exam.

What Medicare does not cover here

As on most commercial plans, laser therapy, shockwave, PRP and other regenerative injections are generally out-of-pocket. We tell you which parts of a plan are covered and which are not before anything starts.

Your own costs depend on your plan, any supplemental coverage and whether the provider accepts assignment.

Before You Book

How to check your own plan

Coverage varies by carrier and by the specific plan you hold, so the only answer worth acting on is the one checked against your policy. We do that for you:

  • Give us your insurance details when you book. Our billing team verifies your benefits before your visit.
  • You hear the numbers up front. The financial conversation happens before treatment, not in the mail three weeks later.
  • Referrals: most patients don't need one to see a podiatrist. Some plans, certain HMOs in particular, do. Call and we'll help you check.

The fastest route is a phone call: (561) 915-1934. Have your insurance card handy and we can usually tell you where you stand on the spot.

Insurance questions we get most

What insurance plans do you accept?

Dr. Cedeno and Dr. Mustafa are credentialed with Medicare, Medicare RR, Aetna, BCBS, Cigna, UHC, Coventry, Dimension, Florida College System, Multiplan / PHCS, Medicarease, Prime Health Services, CorVel (WC), Rockport (WC) and DOL. Does Medicare cover podiatry?

Yes, when the care is medically necessary. Medicare Part B covers a podiatrist’s exams and treatment for foot injuries and diseases, such as hammertoes, bunion deformities and heel spurs. After the Part B deductible ($283 in 2026) you usually pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount. Routine foot care, like nail trimming and corn or callus removal, is usually not covered. The full Medicare guide is above.

How often does Medicare pay for a diabetic foot exam?

Every 6 months, if you have diabetic peripheral neuropathy with loss of protective sensation, as long as you have not seen a foot care professional for another reason between visits. You pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after the Part B deductible.

Does Medicare pay for diabetic shoes?

Medicare Part B covers one pair of extra-depth shoes with 3 pairs of inserts, or one pair of custom-molded shoes with inserts plus 2 more pairs of inserts, each calendar year, if you have diabetes and severe diabetes-related foot disease. The doctor who treats your diabetes must certify the need, a podiatrist or other qualified doctor must order them, and the supplier must be enrolled in Medicare. You pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after the deductible if the supplier accepts assignment.

Does insurance cover podiatry treatment?

Your evaluation, imaging, and standard podiatric care are typically covered, including diabetic foot care and most surgical procedures when needed. Laser, shockwave, PRP and regenerative injections are usually classified as elective and are out-of-pocket.

Does insurance cover laser or regenerative treatments?

Evaluations and most standard podiatric care are typically covered by insurance. Laser, shockwave, and regenerative therapies are usually elective (out-of-pocket) services. Our team verifies your benefits and explains all costs before treatment starts.

Can I use an HSA or FSA?

Physician-prescribed treatment for a medical condition is generally HSA and FSA eligible. We provide the documentation you need to submit.

Do I need a referral to see a podiatrist?

Most patients do not need a referral. Some insurance plans, certain HMOs in particular, require one. Check your plan, or call (561) 915-1934 and we'll help you verify.

How do I find out what my plan covers?

Give us your insurance details when you book and our billing team verifies your benefits before your visit, so the financial conversation happens up front rather than arriving in the mail three weeks later. Call (561) 915-1934.

What should I bring to my first appointment?

A photo ID, your insurance card, a list of medications, any prior imaging or reports related to your foot problem, and the shoes you wear most.
