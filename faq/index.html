MLS Laser Therapy

What is MLS laser therapy?

MLS (Multiwave Locked System) laser therapy is an FDA-cleared treatment that delivers two synchronized wavelengths of laser light into injured tissue: 808 nm to resolve inflammation and swelling, and 905 nm to relieve pain. The energy stimulates your cells' own repair machinery — a process called photobiomodulation. Full details here.

Does laser therapy hurt?

No. Treatment is painless — most patients feel nothing, or a mild soothing warmth. No needles, no incisions, no numbing required, no soreness afterward.

How long does each session take?

10–15 minutes for most foot and ankle treatments. With check-in, you're usually in and out of the office within half an hour.

How many sessions will I need?

Typical plans run 6–12 sessions at 2–3 visits per week. Acute injuries often need fewer; chronic conditions like neuropathy usually need more. You'll get a specific number at your evaluation — and we adjust based on your actual progress, not a fixed script.

How fast will I notice results?

Many patients feel a difference within the first 1–3 sessions. The effects are cumulative, so improvement typically builds through the course — and continues for weeks afterward as tissue keeps remodeling.

Is MLS laser therapy FDA approved?

MLS laser therapy is FDA-cleared for treating pain and inflammation — meaning the FDA reviewed the device and found it safe and equivalent to legally marketed predicates for its intended use. ("Approved" is technically reserved for a different FDA pathway; "cleared" is the correct term for therapy lasers, and any clinic saying otherwise is being imprecise.)

Are there side effects?

Clinical use of MLS laser spanning many years has reported no significant side effects. You may occasionally feel mild, short-lived sensations as circulation increases. It's one of the safest treatments in musculoskeletal medicine.

Who is NOT a candidate for laser therapy?

We don't treat over active cancer, over a pregnant uterus, or near the thyroid or eyes. Certain photosensitizing medications call for caution. Dr. Cedeno screens your history before any treatment starts.

How is MLS different from cheap "cold lasers"?

Three ways: power (class IV delivers a therapeutic dose to deep tissue; low-level devices often can't), dual synchronized wavelengths (the patented MLS pulse), and robotic delivery (computer-controlled, reproducible dosing instead of a hand-held wand). See our technology page.

Can I combine laser with other treatments?

Yes — laser is often the backbone of a combined plan with shockwave, PRP, orthotics, or rehab exercises. Combining correctly is exactly what our regenerative programs are designed to do.

Is there any downtime after a session?

None. Drive home, go back to work, take your evening walk. Laser therapy doesn't damage tissue — it energizes it.

Shockwave & Regenerative Medicine

What is shockwave therapy (EPAT)?

A non-invasive treatment that sends acoustic pressure waves into chronically injured tissue, breaking down scar tissue and provoking a fresh healing response. It's one of the best-evidenced non-surgical options for chronic plantar fasciitis and Achilles tendinopathy. More here.

Does shockwave hurt?

You'll feel firm, rapid tapping — mildly uncomfortable for some, very tolerable for most, and intensity is adjusted to you. Sessions take 10–15 minutes with no downtime.

Laser vs. shockwave — which do I need?

Rule of thumb: laser calms and accelerates (great for pain, inflammation, and most healing), shockwave provokes and rebuilds (great for old, scarred, stubborn tissue). Chronic cases often benefit from both in sequence. The exam determines the right recipe.

What is PRP and how does it work?

Platelet-rich plasma: we draw a small blood sample, spin it to concentrate the platelets, and inject that growth-factor-rich concentrate into your injured tissue. It re-ignites healing in degenerated fascia and tendons. More here.

Is PRP better than cortisone?

They do different jobs. Cortisone suppresses inflammation fast but temporarily, and repeat use can weaken tissue. PRP builds tissue and takes weeks — but its results tend to last. If cortisone keeps wearing off, that's usually the sign to switch strategies.

Are stem cell treatments FDA approved?

No regenerative cell therapy for orthopedic conditions is FDA-approved; these are elective treatments offered under physician judgment where evidence is promising but still developing. We're transparent about this — you'll get the honest state of the science for your diagnosis before deciding. More here.

How long until PRP or regenerative injections work?

These are healing treatments, not painkillers: expect first improvements at 4–6 weeks and continued gains for 3–6 months as tissue remodels. Laser therapy is often added to support and smooth this phase.

Can regenerative medicine help me avoid surgery?

In well-selected cases, yes — that's its best use. In poorly selected cases it wastes your money, which is why candidacy here is decided by a surgeon with no incentive to steer you away from an operation you actually need. You'll get a straight answer.

Conditions & Symptoms

Why does my heel hurt with the first steps in the morning?

That's the signature of plantar fasciitis — the fascia tightens overnight and those first steps re-stretch injured tissue. It's the most common cause of heel pain and among the most treatable conditions we see.

Do heel spurs need to be removed?

Rarely. Spurs appear on X-rays of plenty of pain-free feet; the pain almost always comes from the soft tissue (fascia), not the bone. Treat the fascia and the pain resolves — spur and all. More on heel pain causes.

Can laser therapy really help neuropathy?

For many patients, yes — laser improves the microcirculation that feeds nerve tissue and can meaningfully reduce burning, tingling, and nighttime symptoms. It won't resurrect completely dead nerves, and we're honest about that. Earlier treatment = better odds. Full neuropathy guide.

Is it OK to exercise with plantar fasciitis or tendonitis?

Usually yes, in modified form — tissue heals with the right load, not zero load. We'll tell you exactly what to keep, what to pause, and what to substitute so you don't lose your fitness while you heal.

My ankle sprain was months ago. Why does it still bother me?

Because sprains heal "loose" without rehab — the ligament lengthens and your balance reflexes stay dulled, leading to that give-way feeling. It's fixable at any stage. See sprains & instability.

When is foot pain an emergency?

Seek prompt care for: inability to bear weight, a felt "pop" with sudden weakness, spreading redness or red streaks, fever with foot pain, a cold/blue foot, or any wound on a diabetic foot. When in doubt, call us — we'll tell you where you need to be.

I have diabetes. How often should my feet be checked?

Annually at minimum; every 3–6 months with neuropathy, circulation issues, deformity, or ulcer history. Regular podiatric care measurably reduces amputation risk. Diabetic foot care.

Cost & Insurance

How much does laser therapy cost?

It depends on your condition and plan length, and we quote exact package pricing up front — before you commit to anything. Call (561) 624-4800 for current prices. No surprise bills, ever.

Does insurance cover laser, shockwave, or PRP?

Your evaluation and standard podiatric care are typically covered. Laser, shockwave, and regenerative therapies are usually elective/out-of-pocket (most insurers classify them as emerging treatments). We verify your benefits and give you a complete cost picture before starting.

Is laser therapy worth the out-of-pocket cost?

Compare honestly: months of copays, repeat injections, medication costs and side effects, missed activities — or a defined course of treatment aimed at resolving the problem. For most chronic sufferers the math favors fixing it. We'll help you weigh it for your case, and we'll tell you if we think laser isn't the right value for you.

Do you offer payment plans or packages?

Ask our front desk about current packages and payment options when you call — we work hard to keep effective treatment within reach.

Can I use my HSA/FSA?

Laser and regenerative treatments prescribed by a physician for a medical condition are generally HSA/FSA-eligible expenses. Confirm with your plan administrator; we'll provide any documentation you need.

How do I book an appointment?

Most patients don't — you can see a podiatrist directly. A few HMO plans require referrals; call us and we'll help you check your specific plan.
