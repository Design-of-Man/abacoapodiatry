"Heel pain" is a symptom, not a diagnosis, and at least seven different conditions cause it. The right treatment starts with knowing exactly which one you have.

What is causing my heel pain?

Where your heel hurts, and when, narrows the list fast:

This is why "I tried inserts and they didn't work" is so common: inserts treat some of these well and others not at all. An exam by a foot and ankle specialist sorts this out in one visit, often with in-office imaging the same day.

How is heel pain treated?

Once the cause is confirmed, treatment is matched to it, typically starting with the least invasive effective option:

  • MLS laser therapy for fasciitis, bursitis, and nerve irritation, painless and fast.
  • Shockwave therapy for chronic fasciitis and insertional tendon problems.
  • Orthotics and footwear strategy when mechanics are driving the overload.
  • Targeted stretching and strengthening matched to your diagnosis.
  • PRP or other regenerative options for resistant cases.
  • Immobilization for stress fractures, the one heel problem where rest truly is the treatment.

Should I get a cortisone shot for heel pain?

Sometimes. A steroid injection quiets an inflamed plantar fascia within three to seven days, but it does not repair anything, and the relief usually lasts weeks rather than months. We use it when pain is blocking the rehab that actually cures fasciitis, or when you have a deadline, and we cap it at two, rarely three, injections per heel per year. When the pain keeps coming back, shockwave, MLS laser or PRP, which work on the tissue itself, usually come next. When should I see a podiatrist for heel pain?

  • Heel pain that has lasted more than two to three weeks despite better shoes and stretching
  • Pain with every single step that is getting worse week over week, which needs a stress fracture ruled out
  • Burning, tingling or numbness, which points to a nerve rather than the fascia
  • Pain at the back of the heel near the cord, which points to the Achilles
  • Any heel pain if you have diabetes or reduced feeling in your feet
