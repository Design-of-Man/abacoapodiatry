Our Practice

Medicine That Heals, Technology That Helps

Abacoa Podiatry & Leg Vein Center exists because of a simple frustration: too many patients bouncing between temporary fixes (pills, shots, "come back in six weeks") while their actual injury never healed. We built a practice around doing better.

Who we are

Abacoa Podiatry & Leg Vein Center serves Jupiter and the northern Palm Beaches from our office at 4601 Military Trail, Suite 202. Our focus: resolving foot and ankle pain with treatments that stimulate genuine healing (FDA-cleared MLS laser therapy, shockwave, PRP, and regenerative cell therapy) under the direction of a board-certified foot and ankle surgeon.

That surgical foundation shapes everything. Dr. Orlando Cedeno, DPM trained in reconstructive and trauma surgery of the foot and ankle; he can operate when operation is needed. Which is exactly why his recommendation not to operate carries weight. Here, advanced non-surgical treatment isn't a consolation prize. It's a deliberate first choice, made by someone qualified to offer you every option.

What we believe

  • Diagnosis before treatment. The fanciest laser in the world can't fix a misdiagnosed problem. Every plan starts with finding the true pain generator.
  • The least invasive thing that works. Not the least invasive thing, period, the least invasive thing that works. Sometimes that's stretching. Sometimes it's surgery. Usually it's in between.
  • Honesty about evidence. We'll tell you where research is strong, where it's promising, and where it's still emerging, before you spend a dollar.
  • Measured results. Pain and function are tracked visit to visit. Plans that aren't working get changed, not repeated.
  • Respect for your time. On-time appointments, efficient visits, and treatments designed to fit inside a lunch break.

The Difference

Why patients switch to us

"My shots kept wearing off."

Cortisone manages inflammation; it can't rebuild tissue. When relief keeps expiring, you need treatment aimed at healing. That's our specialty.

"I was told surgery was next."

Sometimes it is. But "we've tried nothing else and we're out of ideas" isn't a surgical indication. Patients regularly find they had more options than they were shown.

"Nobody explained anything."

You'll leave every visit knowing what's wrong, why we're doing what we're doing, and what happens next. Informed patients heal better.
